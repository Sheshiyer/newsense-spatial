## Current Assessment

### What Remains Valid

- The wordmark-locking approach.
- The quiet editorial tone.
- The restrained black, stone, bronze, and neutral palette direction.
- The emphasis on tactile surfaces, sculptural materials, and premium layout.
- The move away from generic agency visuals toward filmic atmosphere.

### What Was Too Loose

- Some outputs drifted into mixed portfolio/client imagery.
- The early run did not have the current clarity around:
  - object map
  - fallback modes
  - project-atlas primacy
  - Meshy-first owned-object generation
- The hero stages leaned too much on collage and not enough on reusable object
  logic.

## Extracted Token Families

### Palette

- near-black
- charcoal
- stone
- muted neutral
- aged bronze
- soft warm highlight

### Material Language

- brushed metal
- oxidized bronze
- stone or mineral matte
- smoked glass
- paper or matte editorial substrate

### Layout Language

- strong margins
- calm grids
- no crowded copy over active imagery
- cover-slide clarity
- quiet premium restraint

### Motion Language

- slow reveal
- heavy-to-light camera movement
- ambient drift instead of playful bounce
- object-first emphasis, not particle noise for its own sake

## Allowed Usage Rules

### Allowed For `Stage`

- palette
- mood
- material cues
- spacing logic
- tonal lighting references

### Allowed For `Studio`

- editorial composition
- typography balance
- tone of presentation

### Allowed For `Projects`

- only as system reference
- never as a replacement for actual project media

### Allowed For Meshy Prompt Packs

- abstract materials
- silhouette adjectives
- surface combinations
- cinematic set-language cues

### Not Allowed

- using client-specific imagery as primary brand identity prompts
- reconstructing the homepage directly from a collage frame
- treating generated slides as canonical logo source
